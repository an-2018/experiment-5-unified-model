## Summary of the submission

The paper asks whether graph-guided MoE routing (KNN graph + GraphSAGE router modulating MMoEEx gates in log-space) improves joint multimodal learning across depression (DAIC-WOZ), sentiment/emotion (CMU-MOSEI), and personality (ChaLearn FI). Across five leakage-safe graph configurations, a K-sweep, and a learned routing weight, the answer is no; a homophily diagnostic localizes why (no label signal in the DAIC graph; unexploited signal in MOSEI/FI). LLM-based encoders dominate everything else tried. Additional sections cover domain adaptation (also negative), MPDD transfer, calibration, and explainability.

## Strengths

The negative result is genuinely well-investigated rather than merely reported. The homophily diagnostic (Table 2 / Figure 4) is exactly the kind of mechanism-level explanation reviewers ask for and rarely get, and it makes a per-task distinction (signal absent vs. signal unused) that gives the null result diagnostic content. The leakage-safe graph construction protocol with an explicit audit is a real methodological contribution — most graph-over-samples papers in clinical ML silently leak. The KNN-voting baseline beating every learned router is an uncomfortable, honest inclusion. Statistical hygiene (BCa bootstrap, DeLong, permutation tests, effect sizes) is above average for this subfield, and the candor about the L4 checkpoint mismatch and the hallucinating GraphXAIN narratives is commendable. The CFP does explicitly admit critical contributions, i.e. principled analyses and arguments that draw attention to fundamentally wrong choice of goals, assumptions, or approaches, so a negative-result paper is not disqualified in principle.

## Major weaknesses

**1. The central comparison sits on a below-chance baseline.** The non-graph MMoEEx baseline scores DAIC AUROC 0.493 — chance level. Every graph-routing conclusion about depression is therefore a comparison between systems that have not learned the task at all; distinguishing 0.49 from 0.55 in that regime is comparing noise against noise. A skeptical reviewer's reading is not "graph routing doesn't help" but "the joint multitask setup fails on DAIC, and no routing mechanism could be evaluated on top of it." The paper's own KNN-voting baseline (0.630) and the standalone MPDD logistic regression results reinforce this. The claim the paper is entitled to make is much narrower than the one in the title and abstract: graph routing doesn't rescue a multitask model that is already failing on the clinical task.

**2. Internal contradiction between the headline claim and the paper's own statistics.** The abstract asserts LLM-based encoders "substantially outperform" everything, but the statistical validation section reports that across all LLM ablation pairs, no comparison reaches significance at α=0.05, with tiny effect sizes (d≈0.15–0.17). You cannot have both. Either the LLM finding is the largest performance driver (abstract, conclusion) or it is statistically indistinguishable from L0 (Results). Reviewers will seize on this immediately. Relatedly, the best result anywhere (L3, DAIC AUROC 0.721) is still below the paper's own cited comparable prior work (Zhang et al., AUROC 0.78), so even the positive finding doesn't advance state of the art.

**3. The "multimodal" and "unified" framing is undercut by the paper's own explainability section.** Figure 2 and Figure 8 reveal DAIC is routed text-only, with audio and video contributing exactly zero. So the clinical task at the heart of a "unified multimodal mental health assessment" paper is effectively a unimodal text task inside a multitask wrapper. This should be front-and-center in the framing, not discovered in the SHAP section.

**4. Scope sprawl.** Graph routing, fusion comparison, LLM ablations, domain adaptation, MPDD transfer, calibration, and two explainability mechanisms is six papers' worth of threads compressed into one. Figure 1 is a software-pipeline/phases diagram that reads like an engineering project report, not a research contribution figure. AAAI's page limit will force cuts anyway; the paper needs one thesis. The strongest candidate is: negative result + homophily diagnostic + leakage-safe protocol, with everything else in supplementary.

**5. Reference integrity — this is the critical one.** Several references appear fabricated, misattributed, or corrupted, and every reference carries a leftover "Used for:" internal annotation that should never appear in a submission:

- Ma et al. 2018 is cited with the wrong title and venue (the MMoE paper is "Modeling Task Relationships in Multi-task Learning with Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts," KDD 2018, not ICML).
- MMoEEx is attributed to "Jacobs, Takahashi, and Morency 2024"; the actual MMoEEx paper is Aoki et al.
- Escalante et al. 2020 is cited as the First Impressions dataset but the listed title is a ChaLearn thermal-spectrum challenge — a different thing entirely.
- The Eyben et al. GeMAPS entry contains garbled author text ("and bind."), Dosovitskiy et al. contains a misspelled fabricated author ("Dollborn"), and Kim/Park/Lee 2024 — the paper whose claim you centrally "REJECT" — has details I cannot verify and looks potentially confabulated.

Under AAAI's stated policy, authors assume full responsibility for content, including checking for plagiarism and veracity of all text, and any allegation of plagiarism, whether the result of the use of an LLM or otherwise... will be thoroughly investigated, with sanctions up to institutional notification. Hallucinated references are treated as a research-integrity issue, not a typo. Every single citation must be manually verified against the actual paper before this goes anywhere.

**6. Sloppiness signals.** Broken cross-references ("Section )" appears at least three times), an inconsistency between the DAIC validation set size (35 in Datasets, 50 in Statistical Validation), and the odd anonymization footnote placement. Individually minor; collectively they lower reviewer confidence in everything else.

## Verdict against the rubric

On soundness, the diagnostic work is good but the below-chance baseline and the significance contradiction are disqualifying for the claims as stated. On significance, AAAI states that solid, technical papers that explore new territory or point out new directions for research... are preferred to papers that advance the state of the art, but only incrementally, or only within a narrow sub-area — a well-diagnosed negative result can qualify, but the specific architecture tested (KNN-GraphSAGE routing into MMoEEx gates) is narrow enough that the community lesson is limited, and the paper itself concedes the untried remedies (jointly-trained embedding spaces, graph rewiring) that a reviewer will say should have been tried before declaring the negative. AAAI is a highly selective conference with two-phase reviewing; this paper's surface issues make it a Phase 1 rejection risk before its substance is even weighed.

## What I'd actually recommend

If you want a realistic path to publication: first, fix the references — non-negotiable, before any venue. Second, cut to one thesis: the negative result + homophily diagnostic + leakage protocol, and reframe the claim honestly as conditional on a multitask setup that fails on DAIC (or better: fix the DAIC baseline first, because a negative result over a working baseline is publishable and this one isn't).
